# Output Template

## Structure

Every paragraph follows the STAR structure: Situation → Task → Action → Result. Each section has stems to start from. Adapt the stem to the user's actual content — do not copy it verbatim.

The paragraph is one continuous block of prose, not four labelled sections. STAR is the internal skeleton, not the visible format.

## Accomplishment stems

### Situation (1–2 sentences)
Set the context: what was the state of the product, team, or problem before you acted.

- "In [quarter/year], [the product/area] was [describe the problem or opportunity]."
- "At the start of [project name], [describe the condition that created the need for the work]."
- "User research showed that [specific insight] — [describe the gap or friction this created]."

### Task (1–2 sentences)
State what you were specifically responsible for.

- "I was responsible for [specific scope of work]."
- "My role was to [specific deliverable or outcome to reach], working with [teams involved]."
- "I led [specific activity] end-to-end, from [start point] to [end point]."

### Action (2–4 sentences)
Name the specific things you did. Each sentence is one action. Use active verbs.

- "I designed [specific artifact] and ran [specific method] to validate the direction."
- "I synthesised [specific input] into [specific output] and presented it to [stakeholder]."
- "I coordinated [specific cross-functional activity] to move the project from [state A] to [state B]."
- "I iterated on [artifact] based on [specific feedback source] before handoff."

### Result (1–2 sentences)
State the outcome. Lead with a metric if one exists. If no metric, name the concrete change that happened.

- "This resulted in [metric] — [brief interpretation of what that means]."
- "The outcome was [specific change], which [describe the user or business effect]."
- "We shipped [artifact] in [timeframe], [describe the direct result]."

## Growth area stems

Growth areas follow a modified STAR structure: Situation (what I noticed) → Task (what I wanted to change) → Action (what I did about it) → Result (what changed or what I'm working toward).

### Situation
- "I noticed that [specific gap or pattern in my work]."
- "Feedback from [source] highlighted that [specific area] was something I needed to develop."

### Task
- "I wanted to [specific skill or behaviour to build]."
- "My goal was to [specific change in how I work or what I produce]."

### Action
- "I [specific thing I did to address the gap] — for example, [concrete instance]."
- "I sought out [specific resource, mentor, or experience] to build [specific skill]."

### Result
- "As a result, [describe what changed in your work or output]."
- "I'm still developing this — my next step is [specific planned action] by [timeframe]."

## Rules

- Cap the paragraph at 15 lines.
- Write in first person throughout. No "we" as the subject of an action you took.
- Do not start two consecutive sentences with "I". Vary the sentence structure.
- Do not use the value names as adjectives in the paragraph ("I demonstrated We Deliver by..."). Show, don't label.
- If a STAR section has no content from the interview, ask the user for it. Do not invent it.

## Example — Accomplishment

> In Q1 2026, the restaurant listing page was underperforming — conversion was declining and user research pointed to friction at the discovery stage. I was responsible for leading the end-to-end UX redesign of the listing experience, working across product and engineering to move from problem framing to a shippable solution. I designed the new listing page, ran 6 usability tests to pressure-test the direction, iterated on prototypes based on findings, and coordinated closely with engineering through to a March 2026 launch. This resulted in a ~3% GMV uplift, improving restaurant discovery and conversion across the platform.

## Example — Growth area

> I noticed that my stakeholder communication was reactive — I was updating people after decisions were made rather than bringing them in earlier. I wanted to build a habit of proactive alignment, particularly with product leads on projects where design shapes the brief. I started scheduling alignment checkpoints at the start of each sprint and sending short written briefs before key reviews. The shift reduced the number of late-stage design revisions on the Vendor Hub project, and I'm continuing to apply it as a default on all active workstreams.
