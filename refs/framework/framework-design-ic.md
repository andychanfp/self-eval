# Pandora Framework — Product Design IC Track

**Job Family Group**: Product | **Job Family**: User Experience | **Job**: Product Design

Use this file when the user's `job` is "product designer" or equivalent. Read the user's `level` from `user-cache.json`, look up the corresponding column, and frame the paragraph to match the scope and responsibility described there.

## Level overview

| Dimension | IC1 — Associate Product Designer | IC2 — Product Designer | IC3 — Senior Product Designer | IC4 — Staff Product Designer | IC5 — Principal Product Designer |
|-----------|----------------------------------|------------------------|-------------------------------|------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| **Tagline** | Entry-level player | Proficient practitioner | Design leadership begins | Master of their craft | Innovation catalyst |
| **Summary** | Actively honing their craft and developing an understanding of organisational context | Proactively builds relationships and demonstrates strong communication skills | Independent designer with strong relationships and the ability to lead projects | Leading performer who manages the most impactful projects | Relying on their strategic mindset and ability to challenge the status quo |

## Scope

| Level | Scope |
|-------|-------|
| IC1 | Contributes to the impact of an initiative or feature **within one squad**. Influences on squad level. Receives close direction. |
| IC2 | Contributes to the impact of an initiative or feature **within one or more squads**. Influences on squad level and occasionally on domain level and design chapter. May receive direction. |
| IC3 | **Owns** the impact of an initiative within a domain or across squads. Influences across squads or domains and within the design chapter. Self-directed. |
| IC4 | **Leads** the impact of undefined problem spaces **across domains**. Influences at tribe level and meaningfully contributes to design chapter initiatives. |
| IC5 | **Leads** the impact of undefined problem spaces **across tribes or within product lines**. Influences at vertical level and leads initiatives in the design chapter. |

## Responsibility

| Level | Responsibility |
|-------|---------------|
| IC1 | Delivers designs to solve **well-defined problems**. Ensures timely completion and meets quality standards for individual assignments. |
| IC2 | Delivers impactful end-to-end solutions to solve **well-defined problems**. Meets deadlines and ensures quality standards in assigned projects. |
| IC3 | Delivers impactful end-to-end solutions to solve **defined and undefined problems**. Assumes responsibility for cross-functional collaboration and impact. |
| IC4 | Delivers end-to-end solutions of **undefined problem spaces**. Guides junior designers. Coaches and mentors junior designers to support their professional growth. Contributes to product and design strategy in collaboration with domain or chapter lead. |
| IC5 | Delivers end-to-end solutions to **undefined problems**. Teacher, mentor, and role model to other designers. Contributes to product strategy and vision with tribe leads. Defines design strategy and vision with chapter leads. Bar setter on design quality. |

## Accountability

| Level | Accountability |
|-------|---------------|
| IC1 | Accountable for their work, taking ownership of assigned design tasks, ensuring effective solutions. |
| IC2 | Accountable for impactful end-to-end solutions, taking ownership of projects, ensuring quality and impact. |
| IC3 | Accountable for impactful end-to-end solutions in both well-defined and undefined problem spaces. |
| IC4 | Accountable for end-to-end solutions in undefined problem spaces and guiding junior designers. |
| IC5 | Accountable for end-to-end solutions in undefined problems, guiding junior designers, and contributing to product and design strategy. |

## Citizenship

## Acumen & Fluency

| Level | Acumen & Fluency |
|-------|-----------------|
| IC1 | Demonstrates curiosity and eagerness to learn. |
| IC2 | **Business awareness**: Cultivates awareness of basic business principles related to design decisions. Cultivates a growing understanding of technical aspects in design feasibility. |
| IC3 | **Strategic business alignment**: Aligns design strategies with immediate business impact. Develops an advanced awareness of technical considerations in design. |

## Skills map

### Expertise level reference

| Level | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Basic** | Has basic knowledge of the subject and is able to use it by applying the elementary reasoning of concepts and skills in the professional environment, sometimes requiring support for the correct application of those concepts. |
| **Intermediate** | Has solid knowledge of the subject and uses the concepts and methods in new situations to solve problems. |
| **Advanced** | Apply knowledge to perform complex analyzes, disaggregate information, find patterns, determine causes and interpret results. |
| **Expert** | Keeps up to date on the most advanced practices in the field and uses knowledge to compare ideas and solutions with a critical view and propose innovative solutions to problems. |
| **Master** | Use knowledge of past experience or other fields to create new and original value solutions. Match knowledge from different areas and predict results and conclusions. |

#### Interaction Design

Defines the flow and behaviour of the product or service to enhance usability, user satisfaction, and overall user experience. Aspects include: design patterns & UX principles (understanding and correct application), information architecture, content definition and micro-copy, and prototypes at various fidelity levels.

**Basic**
- Has a basic understanding of interaction design concepts and principles.
- Follows standard design patterns to design interactions.
- Iterates on flows and interactions based on feedback, data, and research.
- Understands the importance of crafting content intentionally to realise a goal.
- Recognizes the importance of clear labeling, taxonomies, and other navigational matters.

**Intermediate**
- Possesses solid knowledge of interaction design principles.
- Demonstrates the ability to apply concepts and methods in new situations to solve problems.
- Makes trade-offs in proposed flows and interactions based on known constraints.
- Advocates for a preferred approach to a flow or interaction.
- Anticipates and solves for different entry points, exit points, system states, and user states.
- Defines product or service flows with a more nuanced understanding of user satisfaction and usability.
- Crafts content that supports users through basic flows and interactions.
- Organizes and structures content and functionality into user-understandable buckets.

**Advanced**
- Demonstrates a deep understanding of design patterns, UX principles, and information architecture.
- Delivers elegant solutions to complex interaction problems, including connecting multiple products in an experience.
- Coordinates and upholds the consistency of UX across a related collection of flows and interactions.
- Coordinates/diagrams flows and interactions across channels and platforms.
- Develops standards based on patterns in flows and interactions.
- Assists others in developing competence in interaction design.
- Proposes and implements new cross-functional processes to ensure the quality of shipped work.
- Chooses navigational models appropriate for the content and context (e.g. hierarchical browsing, faceted classification, search).
- Identifies gaps in flows and interactions where copy would improve or clarify the user experience.

**Expert**
- Proposes innovative solutions to problems, showcasing a high level of expertise.
- Can both perform and lead others in designing flows and interactions for complicated experiences, managing their complexity and enabling user control.
- Demonstrates remarkable craft, from micro-interactions to the complexity of multi-channel ecosystems.
- Develops training and mentorship tools to help others improve their interaction design practice.
- Uses a rigorous understanding of user behavior (e.g. path analysis, search analytics, generative user research) to inform solutions.
- Defines standards for content quality.
- Chooses the right kind of content type to guide users through a particular situation (tutorials, manuals, contextual help, etc.).

**Master**
- Recognized as an industry/functional leader in interaction design.
- Demonstrates the ability to train, teach, and coach others effectively.
- Has a positive impact on the entire organization, including outside the organization (where relevant).
- Displays exceptional expertise and leadership in defining the flow and behavior of products or services to enhance the overall user experience.
- Creates experiences that leverage content, particularly in new ways on emerging platforms (e.g. voice interfaces, chatbots).
- Maps cross- and omni-channel information architectures, going beyond the screen to bridge online and offline touchpoints.

#### Visual Design

Defines the presentation layers of the product or service in line with the brand, including the structure of the design (UI design) and the style of the visual elements (visual language). Aspects include: design principles, micro-interactions and motion design, designing for the right medium (web, native apps, POS), and design system adoption.

**Basic**
- Knows the design principles but may be learning to apply them consistently.
- Demonstrates a basic grasp of creating static UI designs.
- Limited exposure to design systems and learns to consistently adhere to brand guidelines.
- May need guidance on incorporating micro-interactions and motion design.

**Intermediate**
- Applies design principles consistently to create visually appealing UI designs.
- Can design for different mediums (web, native apps, POS) with some guidance.
- Demonstrates an understanding of basic micro-interactions and motion design.
- Begins to adopt design system components, consistently applying them into their work.

**Advanced**
- Adheres effectively to product and brand design principles.
- Proficient in designing for various mediums, showcasing adaptability.
- Demonstrates a strong understanding of micro-interactions and motion design, enhancing user experience.
- Actively engages with and contributes to the adoption and evolution of the design system.

**Expert**
- Applies design principles consistently, producing designs that align seamlessly and setting an example for others.
- Expert in designing for different mediums with a deep understanding of their nuances.
- Incorporates advanced micro-interactions and motion design to create a highly engaging user experience.
- Sets an example in shaping and evolving the design system, contributing valuable insights and improvements.

**Master**
- Sets organization-wide standards for design excellence, demonstrating innovation and authenticity.
- Consistently produces designs that not only adhere to but elevate product and brand design principles.
- Mastery in designing for various mediums, with a profound understanding of emerging design trends for others to feel inspired.
- Pioneers groundbreaking micro-interactions and motion design, influencing the chapter.
- Assumes a leadership role in developing and advancing design systems, guiding others in their implementation and evolution.

#### Experience Design

Draws on users' needs, feelings, contexts, and mindsets to assess and design end-to-end, surface-to-core experiences. Goes beyond individual touch points to consider the entire service ecosystem. Aspects include: user journey mapping and service blueprints, omni-channel design, emotional design, and inclusive design and accessibility.

**Basic**
- Understands the importance of user needs, feelings, contexts, and mindsets in designing experiences.
- Can create simple user journey maps or interpret complex ones with guidance.
- Aware of omni-channel design and emotional design principles but lacks practical experience.
- Knows the basic principles of inclusive design and accessibility but may require support to apply them in projects.

**Intermediate**
- Can independently assess and design user experiences, considering the entire service ecosystem.
- Proficient in creating user journey maps and service blueprints to identify pain points and opportunities.
- Capable of considering and designing individual touchpoints for a cohesive omni-channel experience.
- Applies emotional design principles to enhance user satisfaction.
- Actively considers and incorporates inclusive design and accessibility standards in designs.

**Advanced**
- Expert in analyzing and considering a service-wide experience, evangelizing its importance at a domain or tribe level.
- Skilled in analyzing and optimizing the user experience through detailed journey mapping and service blueprints.
- Designs seamless omni-channel experiences, integrating digital and physical touchpoints effectively.
- Excels in emotional design, creating impactful and memorable user experiences.
- Champions inclusive design and accessibility, ensuring experiences cater to a diverse range of users.

**Expert**
- Recognized as a thought leader in experience design within the organization.
- Innovates in the creation of user journey maps and service blueprints, setting new standards for the industry.
- Leads the design of complex omni-channel experiences, setting benchmarks for excellence.
- Pioneers new approaches to emotional design, influencing industry trends.
- Advocates for and advances the practice of inclusive design and accessibility, mentoring others in these areas.

**Master**
- Acknowledged as a DH-wide authority in experience design, influencing industry-wide practices.
- Develops groundbreaking methodologies for user journey mapping and service blueprints, shaping the future of the field.
- Sets visionary standards for omni-channel design, leading transformative initiatives across the industry.
- Renowned for emotional design expertise, inspiring a new generation of designers.
- Leads DH-wide initiatives in inclusive design and accessibility, driving systemic change and setting global benchmarks.
- Ability to get the team to achieve mastery in Experience Design.

#### Problem Discovery

Identify and understand the underlying issues or challenges that need to be addressed through systematic, iterative exploration to uncover root causes and inform strategic decisions. Aspects include: empathy (understanding and evangelising users' perspectives, needs, and pain points), research and analysis (collaborating with UXR or conducting research to gather insights), and context (understanding operational nuances, environmental factors, and relevant patterns).

**Basic**
- Works on well-known problems, utilizing known solutions appropriately.
- Always starts design work by understanding the importance of user perspectives, needs, and pain points.
- Utilizes existing research to inform design solutions but may not fully conduct independent research.

**Intermediate**
- Takes a more significant role in defining and prioritizing problems.
- Challenges and influences the team's understanding of user perspectives, needs, and pain points.
- Collaborates effectively with UX Researchers and Data Analysts to gather information, data, and insights for design decisions.
- Understands and considers broader context, operational and business nuances when addressing problems.

**Advanced**
- Deals with larger, more complex problems.
- Understands and considers broader context, operational and business nuances, including the company's goals and strategy, and relevant patterns during problem discovery.
- Conducts rigorous user research to gather information, data, and insights independently.
- Demonstrates advanced empathy in design, effectively understanding and emphasising user perspectives, needs, and pain points.

**Expert**
- Identifies new problems and opportunities for their team to solve.
- Often sees problems and opportunities that others may overlook.
- Addresses problems that are strategic and business-focused, often without an obvious way forward.
- Utilizes advanced research techniques to deeply understand and empathize with users, advocating for their needs and pain points effectively.
- Exploration goes beyond typical design domain boundaries.

**Master**
- Identifies new problems and opportunities across domains and tribes that significantly change product line roadmaps.
- Exploration goes beyond typical design domain boundaries.
- Scope of work has no boundaries — sees problems and dives deeply to solve them.
- Serves as a mentor, training and coaching others in applying problem discovery methods and tools.

#### UX Communication

Ability to convey ideas, information, and design concepts effectively. Aspects include: written, verbal, and interpersonal communication; storytelling (crafting compelling narratives around design decisions); visual communication (conveying complex ideas in an easily understandable visual format); negotiation (ensuring customer context is considered when viewpoints conflict); and actionable deliverables (engaging artefacts to share solutions, progress, and insights).

**Basic**
- Covers fundamental knowledge of communication concepts.
- Requires support in applying concepts to routine tasks.
- Collaborates with team members for guidance on complex communication issues.
- Primarily handles basic written and verbal communication tasks.

**Intermediate**
- Demonstrates solid knowledge of communication concepts.
- Applies these concepts independently to various situations, including negotiation scenarios.
- Manages routine communication tasks effectively.
- Contributes to crafting narratives and visual communication materials.
- Participates in negotiations, ensuring alignment with design objectives.

**Advanced**
- Applies knowledge to conduct complex analyses in communication, including negotiations.
- Disaggregates information, identifies patterns, and interprets results.
- Leads in crafting compelling narratives for design decisions.
- Proficient in creating sophisticated visual communication materials.
- Independently negotiates design decisions in complex situations, resolving conflicts effectively.

**Expert**
- Stays updated on advanced communication practices, with a focus on negotiation strategies.
- Critically compares ideas and solutions, proposing innovative negotiation tactics.
- Leads in negotiating complex design decisions, considering conflicting viewpoints or constraints.
- Produces strategic and highly persuasive actionable deliverables.
- Can teach and guide smaller teams in advanced communication and negotiation strategies.

**Master**
- Recognized industry/organizational leader in communication and negotiation.
- Trains, teaches, and coaches others effectively and efficiently in negotiation skills.
- Innovates communication and negotiation strategies with a substantial impact.
- Sets industry standards in narrative crafting, visual communication, and negotiation.
- Contributes significantly to the field, positively impacting the organization and potentially the industry.

#### UX Collaboration

Ability to work effectively with cross-functional teams (product, engineering, data, brand) and with specialized roles within the UX chapter (UXR, UX Writing, Design Systems). Aspects include: design critique (constructive and insightful discussions about design solutions); facilitation and moderation (leading workshops to drive alignment and decision-making); evangelising (advocating for user-centered design); and seeing things to the end (design documentation, handoff, quality assurance, and measuring UX impact).

**Basic**
- Understands the importance of working with cross-functional teams and within the UX team.
- Participates in design critiques but may require guidance in providing constructive feedback.
- Assists in facilitation and moderation.
- Recognizes the value of user-centered design but may need encouragement to advocate for it.
- Contributes to design documentation and handoff with support from senior peers to ensure quality assurance.

**Intermediate**
- Works closely with cross-functional teams and various specialized roles within UX.
- Participates actively in design critiques, providing constructive and insightful feedback based on customer needs.
- Leads collaborative sessions and workshops effectively, guiding participants and ensuring a productive output.
- Advocates for user-centered design and actively champions for users to influence decision-making.
- Manages design documentation and handoff processes independently with little to no support, ensuring quality.

**Advanced**
- Works hand in hand and collaborates with cross-functional teams and various specialized roles within UX.
- Leads and facilitates design critiques with a critical view, driving alignment and decision-making based on customer needs.
- Demonstrates expertise in facilitation and moderation, effectively managing time and fostering inclusivity.
- Acts as a strong advocate for user-centered design, proposing innovative solutions to customer problems and bringing alignment from different parties.
- Defines and measures UX impact comprehensively, contributing to the continuous improvement of processes and outcomes.

**Expert**
- Recognized as a go-to person for one's scope and has strong and collaborative relationships with cross-functional teams and various specialized roles within UX.
- Constantly compares ideas and solutions critically.
- Successfully leads and helps others lead and facilitate design critiques with a critical view, driving alignment and decision-making based on customer needs.
- Teaches and mentors others effectively, providing guidance and support to smaller teams or individuals.
- Acts as a thought leader in the field, proposing innovative solutions and driving change within and outside the organization.
- Contributes significantly to the development of best practices in collaboration and user-centered design.

**Master**
- Seamlessly integrates with cross-functional teams, guiding collaborative efforts and fostering a culture of user-centered design.
- Excels in providing constructive design critiques and advocating passionately for the user's needs.
- Ensures projects are seen through from documentation to quality assurance, maintaining high standards and measuring UX impact comprehensively.
- Drives innovation, influences stakeholders, and nurtures inclusive environments where creativity thrives.
- By championing users at every turn and leveraging their deep understanding of collaboration dynamics, they make invaluable contributions, elevating both the quality of design solutions and the success of the organization as a whole.

## How to apply

1. Read the user's `level` from `user-cache.json`.
2. Look up each dimension row for that level.
3. Frame the paragraph so the scope, responsibility, and accountability match the level description.
4. Do not claim a scope larger than the level specifies. If the user's work appears to exceed their level, note it and ask before drafting.
5. For skill-specific claims (e.g. "I led a complex interaction design system"), cross-check the stated depth against the expected expertise level for the user's IC level. Flag if the claim implies a higher level than expected.

## Growth Area Exemplars by Level

Use this section in Step 4b and Step 6 when the output type is "growth area". Present the top 3 most critical items for the user's level as suggested options. These are calibrated to the scope and skill expectations for each level — do not suggest IC4/IC5 growth areas to an IC2.

### IC2 — Product Designer

| Priority | Growth Area | What it looks like in practice |
|----------|------------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | **Craft depth — interaction design** | Moving from applying standard patterns to making confident trade-offs and advocating for a specific approach; anticipating edge cases and system states independently. |
| 2 | **Design communication** | Building ability to craft a clear narrative around a design decision in a critique or stakeholder review — not just presenting work, but defending it with user rationale. |
| 3 | **End-to-end quality ownership** | Taking full ownership of handoff documentation and QA without requiring a senior to check; measuring whether what shipped matched what was designed. |
| 4 | **Domain-level awareness** | Starting to understand how their squad's work connects to broader product goals and business context — not just the feature, but the why behind prioritisation. |
| 5 | **PM collaboration** | Moving from receiving a defined brief to actively asking clarifying questions about the problem — understanding the PM's constraints, success metrics, and trade-offs before designing. |
| 6 | **Data fluency** | Beginning to use analytics dashboards and experiment results to validate design decisions rather than relying solely on peer or PM feedback. |
| 7 | **Engineering collaboration** | Flagging feasibility risks during design (not at handoff); following up post-launch to check whether the shipped product matched the intended design. |
| 8 | **AI-nativeness** | Integrating AI tools into the personal design workflow — using them for generative exploration, research synthesis, or copy drafting — and beginning to understand the UX conventions for AI-powered features (loading states, confidence signals, error recovery). |

### IC3 — Senior Product Designer

| Priority | Growth Area | What it looks like in practice |
|----------|------------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | **Cross-squad initiative leadership** | Moving from owning a single squad's work to leading a design effort that spans multiple squads or a full domain — setting the vision, coordinating, and driving alignment. |
| 2 | **Facilitation and workshop leadership** | Leading design critiques and alignment workshops with confidence; managing time, drawing out quieter voices, and producing a clear decision or next step. |
| 3 | **Experience design breadth** | Expanding from screen-level design to mapping full user journeys and service blueprints; identifying gaps that go beyond the immediate product surface. |
| 4 | **Chapter contribution** | Moving from participating in chapter rituals to actively shaping them — proposing a format, running a session, or contributing a perspective on design craft standards. |
| 5 | **Product discovery leadership** | Proactively identifying opportunity spaces before a PM has defined the problem — bringing user research, behavioural data, or competitive signals to open a discovery conversation rather than waiting for a brief. |
| 6 | **PM collaboration — problem co-authorship** | Co-writing problem statements and success metrics with the PM; influencing prioritisation by connecting user insight to business impact, not just flagging UX concerns after decisions are made. |
| 7 | **Data fluency** | Independently pulling and interpreting product analytics to justify a design direction or challenge a PM's assumption; presenting a data-backed case in a cross-functional review. |
| 8 | **AI-nativeness** | Designing AI-powered surfaces end-to-end — handling prompts, uncertainty states, trust signals, and degraded-mode fallbacks — while also using AI tools to accelerate discovery and prototyping across the squad. |

### IC4 — Staff Product Designer

| Priority | Growth Area | What it looks like in practice |
|----------|------------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | **Deliberate coaching of junior designers** | Shifting from ad-hoc feedback to structured coaching — setting up recurring 1:1s, giving feedback that builds skill (not just fixes the deliverable), tracking growth over time. |
| 2 | **Design strategy contribution** | Moving from executing on strategy to co-authoring it — working with domain or chapter leads to propose a design direction that connects to business goals, not just responding to roadmaps. |
| 3 | **Tribe-level influence** | Expanding visibility beyond one domain to shape decisions at tribe level — aligning with peers across domains, influencing product direction, and being seen as a go-to for complex problem spaces. |
| 4 | **External representation** | Beginning to represent design externally — internal talks, blog posts, or conference submissions — as a foundation for the IC5 expectation of attracting talent and elevating the team's reputation. |
| 5 | **Domain-level data fluency** | Using product and UX data to assess impact across the full domain — not just a single feature — and using those insights to inform strategy conversations with PMs and engineering leads. |
| 6 | **AI-nativeness — domain standard-setting** | Defining how AI tools and AI-powered surfaces are approached across the domain: establishing shared conventions, reviewing AI feature designs for quality and trust, and guiding junior designers in this space. |

### IC5 — Principal Product Designer

| Priority | Growth Area | What it looks like in practice |
|----------|------------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | **Organization-wide design standard-setting** | Moving from contributing to standards to defining and socialising them across the organization — publishing frameworks, running training, and measuring adoption. |
| 2 | **Strategic integration with product and business** | Going beyond design strategy to influence product line vision and business goals directly — partnering with tribe leads and senior product stakeholders to shape what gets built and why. |
| 3 | **Mentorship at scale** | Shifting from mentoring individuals to developing the mentorship capability of other senior designers — coaching coaches, creating systems, and raising the bar for how the chapter develops its people. |
| 4 | **AI-nativeness — org-wide leadership** | Leading the organization's thinking on designing for AI — authoring principles for AI-powered UX, setting quality standards for AI surfaces, and shaping how the chapter builds capability in this space. |

## Example

User level: **IC2**

The paragraph centres on the designer's personal delivery — what they designed, what methods they used, what shipped. It credits the team for context but keeps "I" as the actor. It does not claim ownership of a problem space or strategic direction.

User level: **IC4**

The paragraph includes at least one action beyond craft: guiding a junior designer, contributing to a strategy decision, or shaping the scope of a project. Delivery is still present, but scope extends to the domain.
