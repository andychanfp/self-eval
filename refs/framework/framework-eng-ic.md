# DH Engineering Framework — Individual Contributor Track

**Job Family**: Software Engineering | **Levels**: IC1 (Software Engineer I) → IC6 (Senior Principal Engineer)

Use this file when the user's job is a software engineer or equivalent IC role. Read the user's `level` from `user-cache.json`, look up the corresponding column, and frame the paragraph to match the scope and responsibility described there.

---

## Level overview

| Dimension | IC1 — SW Engineer I | IC2 — SW Engineer II | IC3 — Senior SW Engineer I | IC4 — Staff SW Engineer | IC5 — Principal Engineer | IC6 — Senior Principal Engineer |
|-----------|---------------------|----------------------|---------------------------|-------------------------|--------------------------|----------------------------------|
| **Tagline** | Entry-level practitioner | Proficient contributor | Senior technical leader | Domain-level expert | Tribe-wide technical authority | Group-wide thought leader |
| **Summary** | Developing foundational skills with guidance from seniors | Strong fundamentals, autonomous on features, squad-level contributor | Deep expertise, drives architecture at squad/domain, mentors others | Leads cross-domain architecture, role model and technical lead | Sets tribe-wide engineering standards, pragmatic visionary | Group-wide impact, external tech ambassador |

---

## Scope

| Level | Scope |
|-------|-------|
| IC1 | Works on **individual well-defined features** within one squad. Influences squad-level features. Receives close direction from seniors. |
| IC2 | Works on **multiple features and components** within the squad. Direct influence at squad level; sporadic contribution to chapters. Mostly autonomous. |
| IC3 | Owns **multiple features** impacting at the domain level. Direct influence at domain level; indirect at tribe level. Fully autonomous. Mentors less experienced teammates. |
| IC4 | Owns **complex solutions** impacting at the domain level. Direct influence at tribe level; indirect at entity/vertical level. Drives architecture for own team and interacting components. Responsible for developing others. |
| IC5 | **Tribe-wide impact** on all software engineering aspects. Drives overall technical architecture within the tribe. Leads without formal authority. Collaborates with tribe lead on hardest problems. |
| IC6 | **Group-wide impact** on all aspects of software engineering. Renowned thought leader (depth or breadth). Deep understanding of global-scale architecture. Tech ambassador, speaks at conferences and external events. |

---

## Responsibility

| Level | Responsibility |
|-------|---------------|
| IC1 | Delivers solutions to **well-defined problems** with guidance. Takes defined designs and turns them into code on schedule. |
| IC2 | Delivers solutions to **features spanning multiple components**. Works autonomously, contributes through PRs, estimations, and design feedback. |
| IC3 | Designs and delivers in **ambiguous and complex problem spaces** at domain level. Takes lead on challenging projects requiring team input. Drives architectural decisions. |
| IC4 | Leads design and delivery of **highly ambiguous, significant complexity** problems with long-term impact. Produces new software, refactors, or deprecates existing. Technical lead for the team. |
| IC5 | Drives **technically complex projects** across the tribe. Transforms innovative ideas into working software adopted group-wide. Hands-on leader who identifies the right challenges to jump into. |
| IC6 | Defines and drives **technical OKRs for entity/vertical**. Leads global tech projects ensuring timely delivery. Consults product and engineering management on feasibility, effort, and risk of large-scale projects. |

---

## Architecture

| Level | Architecture |
|-------|-------------|
| IC1 | Understands the design of team-owned services: internal structures, data layer, dependencies, cloud costs, and how changes are tested and released. |
| IC2 | Makes pragmatic time/space complexity tradeoffs with senior guidance. Creates flexible software without over-engineering. Actively discusses high-level tradeoffs and avoids unnecessary dependencies. |
| IC3 | Evaluates systems in their lifecycle and proactively fixes architecture deficiencies. Makes appropriate architectural tradeoffs. Drives architectural decisions on features being developed. |
| IC4 | Drives cross-domain architecture discussions. Simplifies systems and processes across the domain. Owns architectural decisions, including how features interact with other domain and tribe components. Aligns teams toward coherent architectural strategies. |
| IC5 | Drives cross-tribe architecture discussions. Reviews architecture proposals and technology choices in the tribe for scale, reliability, and cost-efficiency. Defines tribe-wide standards and contributes to tech strategy. |
| IC6 | Drives non-functional performance (reliability, cost-efficiency, security) across entity/vertical by adjusting architecture, vendor selection, and operations. Consults leadership on technical feasibility and risk of large-scale projects. |

---

## Technical Competence

| Level | Technical Competence |
|-------|---------------------|
| IC1 | Proficient in a broad range of data structures and algorithms. Basic understanding of development best practices (observability, maintainability, scalability, code quality) with senior guidance. Awareness of critical security risks (e.g. OWASP Top Ten). |
| IC2 | Proficient in a broad range of design approaches. Can train new teammates on how the team's software is engineered, operated, and secured. Good understanding of engineering best practices and security implications in code reviews, RFCs, and ADRs. |
| IC3 | Recognized technical expert who adheres to company technical guidelines and industry best practices and evangelizes them. Drives engineering best practices. Deep knowledge of team frameworks and tools beyond common documentation. Proactively looks for security vulnerabilities during reviews; resolves them within SLAs without supervision. |
| IC4 | Drives cross-domain architecture discussions. Coding practices are exemplary: code organization, clarity, simplicity, error handling, testing, and documentation. Sets the standard for engineering excellence in the domain. Fosters a security-first mindset in the domain. |
| IC5 | Go-to person for complex problems. Owns and initiates tribe-level technical initiatives. Owns operational excellence: reliability, performance, cost, and security of tribe applications. Applies DH security strategy across several squads and fosters security-first mindset. |
| IC6 | Proactively initiates and executes process and technical improvements (scaling, tech debt reduction) across the group. Contributes to defining the company's security strategy and fosters security-first mindset across Delivery Hero. |

## Quality of Work

| Level | Quality of Work |
|-------|----------------|
| IC1 | Writes readable, tested code with automated tests following common software engineering practices. Documents software clearly through readable code and self-explanatory tests. |
| IC2 | Considers the long-term impact of contributions on the codebase. Writes code any engineer unfamiliar with the system can understand. Writes unit and integration tests; contributes to keeping end-to-end test suites useful and reliable. |
| IC3 | Solutions are inventive, secure, scalable, extensible, and cost-effective. Writes software that is easy for others to contribute to. |
| IC4 | Software design is exemplary in efficiency, stability, extensibility, and ability to evolve. Lives a blameless culture focused on learning and continuous improvement. |
| IC5 | Drives technically complex projects ensuring timely, high-quality delivery. Drives resolution during high-severity incidents and ensures learnings and follow-up actions are defined at vertical and group level. |
| IC6 | Proactively initiates process and technical improvements across the group. Drives global tech projects, ensuring on-time delivery. |

## Business Expertise

| Level | Business Expertise |
|-------|-------------------|
| IC1 | Seeks to understand the business context and technologies behind the team's product area. Strives to understand customer pain points. |
| IC2 | Understands business context and technologies behind the team's product area. Understands business KPIs of the tribe, domain, and squad, and how their features impact them. |
| IC3 | Focuses on complex, ambiguous problem areas. Understands the business impact of features and shows good judgment on short vs. long-term technical tradeoffs. |
| IC4 | Brings business and industry context to technology decisions as a key influencer in planning strategy. Understands the financial implications of architectural decisions. |
| IC5 | Drives innovation to create business opportunities. Explores "uncharted territories" proactively. Foresees upcoming technical challenges 1–2 years out. |
| IC6 | Drives innovation to create business opportunities that represent a competitive advantage. Defines and evangelizes group-wide standards and tech strategy. Influences direction of external vendors or non-profit groups (e.g. CNCF). |

## Execution

| Level | Execution |
|-------|----------|
| IC1 | Uses technology to solve straightforward problems with guidance. Takes defined designs and delivers on schedule using appropriate technologies and best practices. |
| IC2 | Efficiently utilizes cloud resources. Provides accurate estimates that contribute to team predictability. Focuses on operational excellence. Troubleshoots and resolves defects quickly. |
| IC3 | Delivers impact early and frequently. Slices complex projects into milestones releasable in shorter cycles. Parallelizes work when possible. Diligently follows incident handling policies as on-call; ensures quality incident reports. |
| IC4 | Hands-on technical leader who splits time between coding, design, and architecture based on greatest impact. Breaks down complex problems into straightforward solutions. Drives incident resolution and reviews post-mortems. |
| IC5 | Identifies the right challenges to jump into hands-on. Drives technically complex projects on time and at high quality. Transforms innovative ideas into widely adopted working software. |
| IC6 | Defines and drives technical OKRs for entity/vertical. Drives global tech projects with on-time delivery. |

## Impact

| Level | Impact |
|-------|--------|
| IC1 | Develops solutions that meet stakeholders' needs and overall team goals. Squad-level impact. |
| IC2 | Solves complex problems spanning more than one component. Squad-level direct influence. |
| IC3 | Evangelizes best practices (clean architecture, eliminating tech debt). Guides future technology choices. Contributes to professional development of colleagues. Ensures team is stronger because of their presence — but does not require their presence to succeed. |
| IC4 | Active participant in hiring, providing high-quality feedback and mentoring junior engineers. Trusted part of domain technical leadership. Educates peers on advanced technical issues, technologies, and trends. |
| IC5 | Supports leaders in defining technical OKRs for tribe and vertical. Participates as a bar-raiser in hiring. |
| IC6 | Group-wide impact. Defines and evangelizes group-wide standards, software delivery performance practices, and tech strategy. |

---

## Collaboration

| Level | Collaboration |
|-------|-------------|
| IC1 | Gets designs and code reviewed. Troubleshoots and seeks support when unable to solve independently. |
| IC2 | Works with stakeholders and peers to address customer and business problems. Actively participates in code reviews, design discussions, team planning, and engineering data reviews. Key role in improving team communication and effectiveness. |
| IC3 | Active contributor to chapter or guild. Brings learnings from other chapters/guilds to the team. Detects endemic problems and escalates appropriately. Resolves root causes of endemic issues, including where own team limits other teams' innovation. |
| IC4 | Mentors teams and individuals within domain. Shares knowledge and presents at guild-level activities. Builds consensus. Solicits differing views and is willing to change direction. Probes assumptions and fosters shared understanding. |
| IC5 | Active contributor to the principal council. Mentors teams and individuals within the tribe. Fosters knowledge sharing across entity/vertical and evangelizes best practices. |
| IC6 | Fosters knowledge sharing across the group. Introduces new learnings from outside the group context. |

## Ownership & Accountability

| Level | Ownership & Accountability |
|-------|---------------------------|
| IC1 | Takes responsibility for inherited and developed code. User-centric perspective; proactive in identifying improvement opportunities. |
| IC2 | Autonomous contributor without technical assistance on squad tools and frameworks. Proposes solutions or delegates to the right owner. |
| IC3 | Takes the lead on challenging projects requiring team input. Key influencer in team strategy. Takes ownership of team architecture, system-wide view, and design guidance. Actively recruits, coaches, and mentors squad members. Inspires continuous improvement using quantitative and qualitative signals. |
| IC4 | Main owner of multiple solutions. Fully understands tribe architecture and the big picture of business workflows. Responsible for developing team and domain members. Improves developer experience within the domain. |
| IC5 | Owns the operational excellence of tribe applications. Owns tribe-wide engineering standards. Drives vertical- and group-level follow-up actions from high-severity incidents. |
| IC6 | Accountable for entity/vertical-wide technical direction and OKRs. |

## Growth Mindset

| Level | Growth Mindset |
|-------|---------------|

## How to apply

1. Read the user's `level` from `user-cache.json`.
2. Look up each dimension row for that level.
3. Frame the paragraph so the scope, responsibility, and impact match the level description.
4. Do not claim a scope larger than the level specifies. If the user's work appears to exceed their level, note it and ask before drafting.
5. For skill-specific claims (e.g. "I led a cross-domain architecture redesign"), cross-check the stated depth against the expected level. Flag if the claim implies a higher level.

## Framing rules by level

| Level | Paragraph must show | Paragraph must not claim |
|-------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| IC1 | Individual craft, squad-level delivery, completing assigned tasks with guidance | Cross-domain influence, architectural decisions, mentoring others |
| IC2 | Feature-level ownership, autonomous delivery, squad-level contributions, some chapter involvement | Domain-level strategy, leading architectural decisions, driving team processes |
| IC3 | Domain-level impact, architectural ownership, mentoring teammates, chapter contribution | Tribe-level strategy, cross-domain architectural standards, principal-level visibility |
| IC4 | Cross-domain architecture leadership, technical standard-setting in the domain, developing others, hands-on leadership | Tribe-wide technical OKRs, group-wide standards, principal council participation |
| IC5 | Tribe-wide technical direction, hands-on delivery on hardest problems, mentoring across the tribe, principal council contribution | Group-wide standards without clear scope, entity/vertical OKR ownership |
| IC6 | Group-wide technical standards and strategy, external representation, entity/vertical OKR ownership | — (full scope) |

## Growth Area Exemplars by Level

Use this section when the output type is "growth area". Present the top 3–4 most relevant items for the user's level. Do not suggest IC4/IC5 growth areas to an IC2.

### IC1 — Software Engineer I

| Priority | Growth Area | What it looks like in practice |
|----------|------------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | **Delivering independently on well-defined tasks** | Completing assigned features from design to production without requiring daily check-ins; meeting estimates consistently. |
| 2 | **Code quality and testability** | Writing code that passes review without structural feedback; consistently including unit tests that cover edge cases, not just the happy path. |
| 3 | **Asking better questions** | Moving from "I'm stuck" to "I've tried X and Y, I think the issue is Z — can you confirm?" before escalating to a senior. |
| 4 | **Understanding the product context** | Being able to explain what the feature they just shipped does for the customer, not just the technical mechanism. |

### IC2 — Software Engineer II

| Priority | Growth Area | What it looks like in practice |
|----------|------------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | **End-to-end feature ownership** | Taking a feature from discovery through to production monitoring — not just coding, but writing the ticket, estimating, coordinating review, and following up post-release. |
| 2 | **Design and architecture contribution** | Actively participating in design discussions; writing or proposing an RFC for a meaningful feature before implementing it, not just after. |
| 3 | **Proactive communication with stakeholders** | Flagging risks and blockers before they block — not waiting for a standup to surface a dependency issue that's been blocking progress. |
| 4 | **Chapter participation** | Attending and contributing to chapter discussions; bringing one learning from outside the squad back to the team. |
| 5 | **Security-conscious coding** | Consistently applying secure coding principles (input validation, auth checks, avoiding hardcoded secrets) without being prompted during code review. |
| 6 | **AI-nativeness** | Using AI tools in the development workflow for code generation, test writing, or documentation drafting — while reviewing AI output critically before committing. |

### IC3 — Senior Software Engineer I

| Priority | Growth Area | What it looks like in practice |
|----------|------------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | **Architectural ownership** | Moving from contributing to architectural discussions to driving them — writing RFCs, presenting tradeoffs, and being accountable for the decision, not just executing on it. |
| 2 | **Mentoring junior engineers** | Moving from pairing ad hoc to structured coaching — following up on a junior's growth, giving feedback that builds skill (not just fixes the PR), and advocating for their promotion readiness. |
| 3 | **Cross-team influence** | Having a meaningful impact on engineering decisions outside own squad — leading a chapter discussion, spotting a cross-squad dependency issue and resolving it, or contributing to a domain-level standard. |
| 4 | **Delivery at scale** | Slicing a large initiative into independently releasable milestones; hitting the first milestone while unblocking others in parallel — not waiting for the full feature to be done before shipping. |
| 5 | **AI-nativeness** | Applying AI tools across the team's workflow: using them for code review automation, test generation, or documentation — and starting to have an opinion on where AI-generated code is and isn't appropriate for the team's stack. |

### IC4 — Staff Software Engineer

| Priority | Growth Area | What it looks like in practice |
|----------|------------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | **Cross-domain technical leadership** | Driving an architectural decision that affects more than one team — facilitating alignment, documenting tradeoffs, and being accountable for the outcome even when you don't control all the code. |
| 2 | **Developing other senior engineers** | Structured mentoring of IC2/IC3 engineers — not just code review, but working with their manager on growth plans, giving feedback tied to the career framework, and advocating for their visibility. |
| 3 | **Hiring process contribution** | Leading structured interviews, writing high-quality candidate assessments, and sharing insights with the tribe on what good looks like — not just participating as an interviewer. |
| 4 | **Technical strategy input** | Bringing business and financial context to an architectural decision — estimating the cost implications of two approaches, not just the engineering tradeoffs. |
| 5 | **AI-nativeness — domain standard-setting** | Defining how the domain uses and reviews AI-generated code: what the bar for AI-assisted PRs is, where AI tooling saves time vs. introduces risk, and mentoring the team on responsible use. |

### IC5 — Principal Engineer

| Priority | Growth Area | What it looks like in practice |
|----------|------------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | **Tribe-wide technical OKR definition** | Partnering with tribe leadership to define measurable technical goals (reliability targets, cost reduction %, migration milestones) — not just executing on them but proposing and owning them. |
| 2 | **Cross-tribe influence** | Starting to shape decisions in other tribes — contributing to a working group, reviewing an RFC from a peer tribe, or driving a shared standard that reduces duplication across verticals. |
| 3 | **External representation** | Speaking at a meetup, publishing a technical blog post, or contributing to an open-source project as preparation for the IC6 expectation of building DH's external tech brand. |
| 4 | **AI-nativeness — tribe-level leadership** | Defining the tribe's approach to AI-augmented engineering: which tools are standardized, what guardrails exist, and how the tribe measures AI's impact on delivery velocity and quality. |

## Example calibration

**User level: IC2**
The paragraph centres on a specific feature the engineer owned end-to-end — what they designed, built, tested, and shipped. It credits the squad for context but keeps "I" as the actor for technical decisions. It does not claim ownership of architectural decisions or domain-level strategy.

**User level: IC4**
The paragraph includes at least one action beyond personal delivery: driving an architectural decision across teams, mentoring a junior engineer, or setting a technical standard for the domain. Hands-on delivery is still present, but the scope extends to the domain and other engineers.
