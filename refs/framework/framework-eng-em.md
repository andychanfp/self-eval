# DH Engineering Framework — Engineering Management Track

**Job Family**: Engineering Management | **Levels**: M1 (Engineering Manager) → M4 (Engineering Senior Director)

Use this file when the user's job is an engineering manager or equivalent EM role. Read the user's `level` from `user-cache.json`, look up the corresponding column, and frame the paragraph to match the scope and responsibility described there.

---

## Level overview

| Dimension | M1 — Engineering Manager | M2 — Senior Engineering Manager | M3 — Engineering Director | M4 — Engineering Senior Director |
|-----------|--------------------------|----------------------------------|---------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Tagline** | Squad enabler | Domain leader | Tribe director | Vertical executive |
| **Summary** | Manages a single squad; technical owner and people developer, building an autonomous self-organized team | Leads multiple squads/a domain; builds second-layer management and drives domain strategy | Leads a tribe of 40+ engineers; sets technical and cultural direction at tribe level | Leads a large tribe or vertical of 80+ engineers; shapes vertical and DH-wide tech strategy |
| **Org size** | 4–10 engineers (recommended 6–8); single squad | 3+ squads, typically within one domain | 40+ engineering roles; a tribe or complex domain | 80+ engineering roles; a large tribe or young vertical |

---

## Scope

| Level | Scope |
|-------|-------|
| M1 | **Single squad** (4–10 engineers). Technical owner of the squad. Direct influence at domain level; indirect at tribe level. Leads with guidance from senior manager or director. |
| M2 | **3+ squads**, normally within one domain. Direct influence at tribe level; indirect at larger organizational level. Leads domain autonomously with product counterpart. Has at least one M1 direct report. |
| M3 | **Tribe or complex domain of 40+ engineering roles**. Direct influence on other tribes and/or the vertical. Leads autonomously and sets direction with product counterpart. |
| M4 | **Large tribe or young vertical of 80+ engineering roles**. Direct influence at vertical level. Leads autonomously; shapes DH-wide tech strategy as an active member of tech leadership. |

## Leadership & People Management

| Level | Leadership & People Management |
|-------|-------------------------------|
| M1 | Responsible for team happiness, engagement, and growth. Holds regular 1:1s with direct reports; provides constructive, structured feedback. Identifies growth opportunities for high and low performers. Conducts high-quality performance reviews. Runs team-building activities. Understands skill gaps and works on growth plans and staffing needs. Contributes to recruitment; ensures smooth onboarding. Leads by example; fosters an inclusive, discrimination-free environment. |
| M2 | Builds a strong delegation framework with reporting managers. Stays close to indirect teams' dynamics. Ensures domain readiness for performance reviews. Establishes a strong second layer by growing internal managers and attracting senior talent. |
| M3 | Expert leader for the tribe/domain. Leads 3–6 senior engineering managers. Establishes own leadership framework and adapts to organizational needs. Takes performance reviews seriously; provides 360 feedback for every tribe member. Creates an engaging and diverse workplace, leading by example. |
| M4 | Expert leader of the tribe or vertical. Normally leads 3–6 senior engineering managers and directors. Aware of the organization's talent debt (skill gaps, seniority gaps, succession, retention) and has a solid strategy to address it. Involves self in people management topics that benefit the overall company. |

Aware of DH's financial context (P&L, balance sheets). Builds tribe/domain strategy and establishes an ambitious OKR and roadmap process. |

## Delivery

## Technical Strategy & Operational Excellence

| Level | Technical Strategy & Operational Excellence |
|-------|---------------------------------------------|
| M1 | Guides squad's technical strategy and operational execution. Accountable for every technical decision. Proficient in the team's tech stack; actively mentors engineers and evolves code reviews. Owns architectural decisions and RFC culture. Establishes excellent operational practices: stability, security, cost-efficiency, performance, observability. Ensures proper on-call, runbook, and incident management. Initiates collaboration and innovation with domain peers. Guides squad to resolve security vulnerabilities within SLAs. |
| M2 | Ensures all engineering practices are documented, lived, and regularly reviewed. Ensures visibility and continuous improvement of technical KPIs. Tracks budget with Director. Defines domain's security approach based on DH security strategy; ensures vulnerabilities are resolved within SLAs. |
| M3 | Owns the quality of decisions in their space. Coaches tribe on excellent incident handling. Owns every tech excellence practice within the tribe, driving quality, productivity, and security. Creates a culture of conscious spending and cloud resource efficiency. Has a future-proof strategy to tackle scalability bottlenecks and tech debt. |
| M4 | Pragmatically incorporates innovations and new industry standards. Develops a strong external network and enhances decision-making with relevant outside input. |

1. Read the user's `level` from `user-cache.json`.
2. Look up each dimension row for that level.
3. Frame the paragraph so the scope, people impact, and organizational influence match the level description.
4. Do not claim a scope larger than the level specifies. If the user's work appears to exceed their level, note it and ask before drafting.
5. For claims about team size, org structure, or strategic scope, cross-check against the org size expectations. Flag if the claim implies a higher level.

## Framing rules by level

| Level | Paragraph must show | Paragraph must not claim |
|-------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| M1 | Squad-level delivery, direct people management, hands-on technical involvement, team health and growth | Domain-level strategy ownership, managing managers, setting tribe-wide technical standards |
| M2 | Multi-squad impact, managing managers (M1 direct reports), domain vision and 18-month strategy, cross-team delivery | Tribe-level P&L awareness, C-level representation, vertical-wide strategy |

## Growth Area Exemplars by Level

Use this section when the output type is "growth area". Present the top 3–4 most relevant items for the user's level. Do not suggest M3/M4 growth areas to an M1.

### M1 — Engineering Manager

| Priority | Growth Area | What it looks like in practice |
|----------|------------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | **Structured feedback and performance management** | Moving from ad-hoc 1:1s to consistent, documented feedback cycles — giving specific, behaviorally grounded feedback tied to the career framework rather than general praise or vague concerns. |
| 2 | **Delivery metrics ownership** | Actively tracking deployment frequency, cycle time, and incident metrics; presenting trends to the team and initiating one concrete improvement per quarter, rather than just reporting numbers. |
| 3 | **Stakeholder management** | Proactively updating stakeholders on delivery risks before they become blockers — not waiting for a status meeting, but driving a clear and regular communication rhythm. |
| 4 | **Technical strategy participation** | Moving from executing on the squad's technical direction to having a clear opinion on it — writing or reviewing RFCs, challenging architectural choices, and being accountable for the decisions, not just implementing them. |
| 5 | **Growing a second-layer lead** | Identifying a senior IC in the squad who could grow toward a tech lead or future EM role; actively investing in their growth and giving them structured opportunities to lead. |

### M2 — Senior Engineering Manager

| Priority | Growth Area | What it looks like in practice |
|----------|------------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | **Domain vision and 18-month strategy** | Moving from reacting to tribe priorities to proactively setting a domain strategy — writing a vision doc, presenting it to the tribe lead, and tracking execution against it quarterly. |
| 2 | **Managing managers** | Building a delegation framework with M1 direct reports — defining expectations, measuring performance through output metrics, and coaching managers rather than doing their jobs for them. |
| 3 | **Cross-domain collaboration** | Proactively identifying and resolving cross-domain dependencies before they escalate — owning the relationship with peer domain EMs, not just attending syncs. |
| 4 | **Talent development at scale** | Demonstrating a track record of promoting senior engineers; having at least one engineer who reached IC3+ or above with direct evidence of the EM's coaching contribution. |
| 5 | **Domain-level technical KPIs** | Defining measurable technical health metrics for the domain (reliability, security vulnerability resolution rate, deployment frequency) and driving visible improvement over 2+ quarters. |

### M3 — Engineering Director

| Priority | Growth Area | What it looks like in practice |
|----------|------------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | **OKR ownership and execution** | Moving from contributing to OKR planning to owning the tribe's OKR process end-to-end — setting ambitious but realistic goals, reviewing progress monthly, and adjusting course with data rather than anecdote. |
| 2 | **C-level communication** | Representing the tribe in discussions with global or local C-level; communicating technical context at the right level of abstraction — not too deep, not too vague. |
| 3 | **Budget governance** | Moving from awareness of budget to active governance — tracking spend against forecast, making trade-off decisions on headcount vs. tooling vs. cloud cost, and presenting a clear budget narrative to leadership. |
| 4 | **Cross-vertical initiative leadership** | Leading or co-leading a significant initiative that spans multiple tribes — not just participating, but driving the execution plan, resolving cross-tribe dependencies, and owning the outcome. |

### M4 — Engineering Senior Director

| Priority | Growth Area | What it looks like in practice |
|----------|------------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | **Group-wide tech strategy contribution** | Moving from vertical-level strategy to actively shaping DH-wide engineering direction — proposing standards, leading a working group, or co-authoring a group-level technical position. |
| 2 | **External network development** | Building a strong external network that feeds decision-making — participating in industry groups, relationships with vendors, or external advisors that bring relevant outside input to vertical strategy. |
| 3 | **Mentoring younger leaders at scale** | Systematically developing director and senior manager talent across the organization — not just within the vertical, but advising other tribe leads on leadership development. |

## Example calibration

**User level: M1**
The paragraph centres on a specific people or delivery outcome the EM drove within their squad — a team health improvement, a hiring success, a delivery milestone. It positions the EM as the enabler of the team's success, not the executor of the work. It does not claim domain-wide strategic ownership.

**User level: M3**
The paragraph includes a strategic action: setting the tribe's technical direction, managing a complex stakeholder relationship with C-level, or driving a cross-tribe initiative. People impact is still present (the EM grew a manager, ran performance reviews), but the scope is the tribe and the framing is organizational, not individual.
